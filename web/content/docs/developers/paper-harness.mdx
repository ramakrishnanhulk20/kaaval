Three paper accounts, one fill model, and a rule that neither may ever invent liquidity. This is the
part a judge should be most suspicious of, so this page states its assumptions before it states its
results.

## The fill model

From the header of `src/sim/fill.ts`, which is where the assumptions live so nobody has to guess
when reading the record:

- A **market order** walks the recorded book level by level and pays the taker rate.
- A **limit order that crosses the spread** does the same but stops at its price.
- A **limit order that does not cross** is treated as resting and filled at its own price for the
  maker rate. This is the one optimistic assumption in the whole project.
- Every fill gives up `extraBps` against the trader on top of walking the book. The engine sets it
  to 5 basis points, standing in for the queue, the latency and the spread that were not measured.
- No order may take more than `maxBookFraction` of the size visible on the side it hits. The engine
  sets it from the rulebook's own gate, a quarter.

The resting-limit assumption is what `maxBookFraction` exists to bound. A quarter of the visible
book keeps the error small. It does not make the assumption true, and the
[trust model](/docs/concepts/trust-model) says so in the same words.

An order the model will not take comes back as a rejection with a reason in words: a quantity that
is not positive, a book for a different symbol, no liquidity on the side it would hit, a fraction
outside zero to one, a negative fee rate.

## The fees

The engine passes the rates in; the simulator never assumes a rate of its own.

| Category | Maker | Taker |
|---|---|---|
| `SPOT` (rTokens) | 0.1 percent | 0.1 percent |
| `USDT-FUTURES` (perpetuals) | 0.02 percent | 0.06 percent |

Bitget's public fee rates, read 8 September 2026. The source string goes into the `config` entry
next to the rates, so a replay years later uses the rates the run used rather than today's.

## The book hash

`canonicalJson` in `src/sim/book.ts` writes every number as a fixed twelve decimal string when that
string reads back as the exact same double, and at full precision when it does not. Two different
numbers can never produce the same text, so a tampered digit always changes the hash, and the same
book produces the same hash on any machine. The hash of the book goes into the fill, and the book
itself goes into the ledger as a snapshot entry.

## The paper account

`src/sim/account.ts` is pure. `applyFill` returns a new account rather than mutating one, so a fill
that the account refuses cannot leave it half updated. It tracks a balance, a map of positions with
average entry, realised profit and loss, and fees paid.

The invariant that matters is the one the fuzz test holds:

> equity equals starting balance plus realised plus unrealised

`test/sim/invariants.test.ts` runs 2,000 random sequences of fills against it. Any drift in the
account math shows up there rather than in a ledger a judge is reading.

## Pointing it somewhere else

The harness has no dependency on the rest of Kaaval. `fillAgainstBook(order, book, fees, model)`
takes an order, a book, a fee schedule and a slippage model and returns a fill or a rejection.
Nothing in it knows about brains, the rulebook, the engine or Bitget. That is why the same functions
run under the fuzz test, under the replay, and in the live tick, and why a replay years from now
does not need a network.
