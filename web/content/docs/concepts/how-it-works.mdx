title: How it works
---

Bitget lists tokenized US stocks that trade 7 by 24. The stock market they track is open about 32
hours a week. So for most of every week there is a price for rNVDA and rSPY with no exchange behind
it, and Bitget's own pages say the weekend quote is a reference price that re-anchors when New York
opens. That is a real gap where a human cannot sit, and where a model left alone with a wallet is a
bad idea.

Kaaval sits in that gap. One rulebook in plain English, three brains that see the identical world
state and trade the identical sizes, a risk layer that is pure code with no network and no model
access, and a signed ledger that makes the whole night checkable after the fact. The brains propose.
The rulebook disposes. The record shows which of them was right.

## One night

The engine wakes on a clock, not on an event. One tick every 15 minutes, one every 2 minutes for an
hour after a scheduled event (an earnings release after the close, the FOMC decision, CPI) and for
the 30 minutes on each side of the regular open.

Each tick runs in this order.

**1. The kill switch is read first.** A file at `data/state/KILL`, or `KAAVAL_HALT=1`, halts new risk
within one tick. A change either way is written to the ledger as a halt entry before anything else
happens.

**2. Perception builds one world state.** Tickers, order books, divergence from the last regular
close, funding rates, open interest, the last six hours of news, the calendar, and the account. The
order books used this tick are written to the ledger as snapshot entries, so every fill that follows
can be recomputed later. All three brains get this same object. A difference in their decisions can
only come from the brain, never from what it saw.

**3. Risk moves before anyone thinks.** Stops that the recorded book has already passed through, and
every open position when the kill switch or the drawdown halt is on, become reduce-only orders and
execute now. A stop must never wait on a model that is thinking.

**4. Each brain decides.** `decide(world, account, rulebookText)` returns targets and a written
rationale, never orders. A target is a symbol, a signed size in USDT, what it hedges if anything, a
rationale, a confidence and a horizon. The decision goes into the ledger as written, including the
model calls, the token counts and the time it took. A brain that throws or runs past its deadline
gets a decision entry with an error and no targets, so a model that failed looks like a model that
failed and not like a quiet night.

**5. The rulebook judges every target.** `risk/limits.ts` turns targets into intents, adds the
hedges the cross-asset rule demands, and returns a verdict for each one. Size caps cut an order down
and record the cut. Everything else refuses the order outright and records the rule that refused it.

**6. Execution sizes what survived.** It rounds to the instrument's own precision, picks limit or
market, and renders the order through the Agent Hub SDK as a dry run, so the exact request Bitget
would have received sits in the ledger next to the fill. The stop for the new position is written
the same way, as the `tpsl` strategy-order call that would arm it.

**7. The fill.** Stock legs fill in our simulator against the order book recorded in step 2, at the
fee rates and slippage model in the config entry. Crypto hedge legs go to Bitget's demo environment
when a demo key is present, and the response comes back in the ledger. Either way a fill entry is
written.

**8. The mark.** Equity, balance, realised, unrealised, drawdown, profit on the day and every open
position, per brain, written to the ledger. The equity curve you see on the site is nothing but
these entries in order.

Then the clock schedules the next tick.

## What a night costs

Observed on 11 and 12 September 2026, from the engine log and the decision entries in the ledger.

- **36 Bitget reads per tick** in the steady state, on a universe of 14 symbols: 6 rTokens, their 6
  stock perpetuals, plus BTC and ETH. One ticker and one book each, funding on the 8 perpetuals. The
  divergence anchor costs 6 more candle reads once per New York close, not per tick, because it is
  cached against the close it is measured from. All public reads, no key.
- **3 model calls per language-model brain per tick**, dropping to 2 in the event and open windows.
  The median Claude prompt was 9,594 tokens and the median completion 6,183.
- **51 to 124 seconds of wall clock per tick** across the last eight ticks in the log. The median
  Claude decision took 67.7 seconds and the slowest 99.1.
- Market data is free and public, so the only cost of running Kaaval is the model calls.

## What Kaaval does not do

It does not hold money, place a live order, or ask you for a withdrawal permission. It does not
learn: the rulebook is data a human edits, and a change to it is a config entry in the ledger. It
does not decide when to stop trading by asking a model; that is a file on disk and a percentage in
a config file.
