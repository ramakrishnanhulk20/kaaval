Take Bitget out of Kaaval and there is nothing left. Not a slower version: nothing. The instruments
exist there, the data comes from there, the safety gate on every order is theirs, and the one rule
that makes this project interesting only has something to hedge with because Bitget lists both legs
under one account.

## The instruments: rTokens and stock perpetuals

Bitget lists tokenized US stocks as spot pairs, the rTokens, and separately lists stock perpetual
futures. Over a hundred of the rTokens trade 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, while the exchange they
track is open about 32 hours a week.

That pairing is the whole product. An rToken position that is open over a weekend carries a price
that no exchange is standing behind, and Bitget's own guidance says that price re-anchors at the
open. The cross-asset rule in the [rulebook](/docs/guides/read-the-rulebook) answers it: any rToken
position above 5 percent of equity must carry a hedge of at least half its delta, in its own stock
perpetual or in SPY, whenever divergence is over 0.5 percent or New York will be shut for more than
24 hours.

You cannot write that rule anywhere else, because nowhere else has both legs.

## The Agent Hub SDK

Every read Kaaval makes goes through `@bitget-ai/bitget-agent-sdk` 3.3.0, not raw HTTP. Seven market
actions build the world state, and the order desk uses the SDK's own order verb. The
[Bitget verbs page](/docs/developers/bitget-verbs) lists each call and where it is used.

Two SDK flags do work our own discipline should not be trusted with:

- `readOnly` on the market context. The client that reads prices physically cannot place an order,
  enforced at the SDK layer.
- `paperTrading` and `dryRun: true` on the order desk. Every order is rendered by the SDK's safety
  layer as the request Bitget would have received, and that request is written into the ledger next
  to the fill. The SDK answers a dry run before it looks at a credential, so this is Bitget's own
  rendering of the request, not our guess at it.

When the SDK will not preview a request, the fallback is written from the order tool's own schema
and a config entry in the ledger says the preview was built locally. The record never quietly
upgrades a locally built request into a Bitget one.

## The Agentic account

Bitget's Agent Hub ships an OAuth flow, `authorize_start`, `authorize_wait` and `get_auth_status`
in the SDK, that mints an agent its own sub-account with its own quota, fund isolation and no
withdrawal permission. That is the right home for a strategy like this one when it goes live: the
agent gets a pot, and the pot is all it can ever touch.

Kaaval does not run on one today, and this page is not going to pretend otherwise. The flow needs a
browser sign-in by the account owner, and the quota and isolation guarantees live on Bitget's
servers where we cannot read them from code. What Kaaval does instead is keep the path short: the
brains produce targets, `risk/` judges them, and exactly one function builds an order. Pointing that
function at a credentialed Agentic session is a configuration change, not a rewrite.

## The demo environment, and its limit

Bitget runs a demo trading environment with its own keys. Kaaval uses it for the crypto hedge legs:
`SBTCSUSDT`, `SETHSUSDT` and `SXRPSUSDT`. When the three demo variables are set, those orders are
placed for real against Bitget's own matching, and the response is written into the ledger.

The demo environment has no stock symbols. There are exactly three crypto contracts on it. That is
why the stock legs are simulated against a recorded book instead of paper traded: there is no paper
venue for a tokenized stock, on Bitget or anywhere. It is the single biggest honest limitation of
this project and it is stated on every screen that shows a number, in the
[trust model](/docs/concepts/trust-model) and in the [threat model](/docs/security/threat-model).
