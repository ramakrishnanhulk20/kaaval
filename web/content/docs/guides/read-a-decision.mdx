**Where:** `/decisions/<seq>`, where `<seq>` is the ledger sequence number of the decision. Every
decision on the timeline and every last-decision summary on the scoreboard links to one.

A decision page is one brain at one tick, from what it saw to what it got.

## The four parts

**1. What it proposed.** Every target: the symbol, the signed size in USDT (positive is long,
negative is short, zero is flat), what it hedges if anything, the brain's own rationale for that
target, its confidence and its horizon in minutes. Above them, the brain's one-line summary of the
tick, and the machinery: how many model calls it made, the prompt and completion tokens, and how
long it took.

**2. What it saw.** The world state at that tick, taken from the snapshot entries written just
before the decision: the symbols in tonight's universe with their divergence from the last regular
close and their spread. This is the part that makes the page worth reading. The targets are an
opinion; the world is a fact, and it is stored, so you can judge the opinion against it.

**3. What the rulebook said.** A verdict per target. Allowed, allowed after being cut down to a cap
with the cut recorded, or refused with the rule named and the reason in words. Refusals that were
not tied to any target the brain proposed, a required hedge that could not be placed for example,
are listed separately so the page does not imply the brain asked for them.

**4. What happened.** The Agent Hub dry-run request built from whatever survived, exactly as it went
to the SDK, and the fill it produced: price, quantity, fee, slippage, and the hash of the book it
was priced against.

## Reading it well

- A refused decision is as informative as a filled one. The rule name tells you which limit bit.
- `modelCalls: 3` means the ensemble ran three times on that tick. A `2` means it was an event or
  open window, where the cadence tightens and each brain gets fewer runs.
- A decision with an error and no targets is a brain that threw or ran past its 180 second deadline.
  It is in the record because a model that failed should look like a model that failed.
- The rationale is the model's own account of itself. It is published, not endorsed. See the
  [trust model](/docs/concepts/trust-model).
