title: Check the refusal log
---

**Where:** `/refusals`.

Every order the rulebook said no to, newest first, across all three brains. Each row carries the
rule that refused it, the reason in words, the brain, the symbol and the time. Filter by rule or by
brain.

This is the page that shows the risk layer working, so it is worth more than the fills page.

## The rule names

A refusal is written to the ledger with the name of the rule that produced it, so the counts are
countable. The names follow the shape of the rulebook:

| Rule | What it catches |
|---|---|
| `input.notionalUsdt` | A size that is not a positive number, checked before anything else |
| `universe.symbol` | A symbol that is not in tonight's universe |
| `universe.tradable` | A market that is shut, which refuses every order, the kill switch flattening included, because a closed market cannot fill |
| `exposure.perSymbolPct`, `exposure.grossPct`, `exposure.netPct` | The three exposure caps, when the order cannot be cut down to fit |
| `exposure.maxPositions` | The ninth open position |
| `exposure.minOrderUsdt` | An order below the 100 USDT floor |
| `loss.dailyLossPct` | New risk after the day is 2 percent down |
| `loss.drawdownHaltPct` | New risk after the 12 percent drawdown halt |
| `gates.maxSpreadBps` | A spread wider than 30 basis points |
| `gates.maxDivergencePctForNewLong` | A new unhedged rToken long more than 1 percent from the last regular close |
| `gates.noEntryWindow` | An entry between 09:25 and 09:40 ET, when Bitget's guidance says prices re-anchor |
| `gates.preOpenDivergencePct` | A new unhedged rToken long in the last 30 minutes before the open with divergence over 0.5 percent |
| `safety.kill` | The kill switch was on |
| `execution` | The order survived the rulebook but could not be built or filled: no quote or no book this tick, a size that rounds to nothing, more than a quarter of the visible book, or the account refusing the fill |

Two limits never appear here. `exposure.maxOrderUsdt` cuts an order down to the 500 USDT ceiling
and notes the cut on the order entry instead of refusing it, and the cross-asset hedge rule adds a
hedge order rather than refusing the position; a hedge whose perpetual could not be read this tick
is refused with that written down, never quietly swapped for SPY.

An `execution` refusal is not a rulebook refusal. It means the world got in the way after the
rulebook had already said yes, and the reason field says which way.

## What to look for

- **Two refusals under `safety.kill` on 11 September, between 08:42:20Z and 08:59:09Z.** That is the
  kill switch working on a live night, not in a test.
- **Size refusals versus size cuts.** An order below the 100 USDT floor is refused. An order above
  the 500 USDT ceiling is cut down to the ceiling and allowed, and the cut is recorded on the order
  rather than here.
- **A rule that never fires.** If a limit has zero refusals across the whole record, either nothing
  has tested it yet or it is set too loose to matter. The counts per rule at the top of the page are
  there so you can see which is which.

Reduce-only orders, stops and the kill switch flattening the book skip the entry gates and the order
size band on purpose. Refusing to shrink a position is how a risk layer traps a loss instead of
stopping one.
